## The one piece of gear that matters

You can run in almost anything, but the shoes are the exception. They are the only thing between you and the ground for thousands of footstrikes per run, and the wrong pair is the fastest route to a sore knee or shin. The good news: choosing well is simpler than the wall of options in the shop suggests.

## Neutral versus stability

Most marketing language collapses into two families.

- **Neutral** shoes have even cushioning and no built-in correction. They suit the majority of runners, whose feet roll inward a normal, healthy amount.
- **Stability** shoes add firmer material on the inner edge to resist excessive inward roll (overpronation). They help a minority of runners who genuinely need the support.

Do not over-think this. Unless you have a history of specific injuries or a gait analysis telling you otherwise, start neutral and comfortable. The "support you need" industry oversells correction; comfort is a far better predictor of staying injury-free than any pronation category.

## Getting the fit right

- **Size up.** Running feet swell. Leave a thumb's width between your longest toe and the end of the shoe — often half a size or a full size larger than your street shoes.
- **Try them late in the day** when your feet are at their largest, and wear your running socks.
- **Walk and jog in them.** A good running shop lets you test on a treadmill or outside. If anything pinches or rubs in the shop, it will be worse at kilometre eight.
- **Ignore the colour.** Fit first, looks last.

## When to replace them

Cushioning compresses and stops absorbing impact long before the upper looks worn out. As a rule of thumb, a pair lasts **500 to 800 kilometres**. Heavier runners and harder surfaces wear them faster. The tell-tale signs are new aches in the knees, hips, or shins that arrive with no change in your training — often the shoes, not your legs, have given out.

The simplest way to know when you are due a new pair is to track the distance on each pair as you run. Logging your gear means the app can warn you as a shoe approaches the end of its life, so you replace it before the aches start rather than after.
