## What road running actually is

Road running is the simplest endurance sport there is: you, a pair of shoes, and a stretch of pavement. There is no equipment to master and no court to book. That simplicity is exactly why it is the easiest fitness habit to start — and the easiest to start badly. Most people who quit in the first month do so because they ran too fast, too far, too soon, and decided running "isn't for them." It is. You just need a gentler on-ramp.

## What you actually need

You need far less than the shops would like you to believe.

- **Shoes that fit.** One properly-fitted pair of running shoes. Not the cheapest, not the most expensive — comfortable, the right size, and replaced every 500–800 km. That is the whole equipment list for your first three months.
- **Clothes you can move in.** Anything that wicks sweat and does not chafe. You do not need a matching kit.
- **A way to track time.** A watch or a phone. Early on you will run by minutes, not distance, so all you need is a clock.

Everything else — heart-rate straps, carbon plates, fuelling gels — is for later, if ever.

## Your first four weeks

The single most important idea for a new runner is **easy means easy**. If you can comfortably hold a conversation while running, you are going at the right pace. If you are gasping, you are going too fast — slow down, even to a walk. Walking is not failure; alternating running and walking is how nearly everyone builds their base.

A sensible first month looks like three sessions a week, each 20–30 minutes, alternating gentle running with walking. Add a little more running and a little less walking each week. By the end of the month most people can run continuously for 20 minutes at an easy pace. That is a real milestone, and it is enough to build everything else on.

Rest matters as much as running. Take at least one full rest day between your first runs — your tendons and joints adapt more slowly than your lungs do, and the most common beginner injuries come from doing too much before the body has caught up.

## Building a habit that sticks

Consistency beats intensity every single time. Three easy runs a week, every week, for a year will transform your fitness far more than a heroic fortnight followed by burnout. Pick fixed days, lay your kit out the night before, and treat the run as an appointment you keep with yourself.

When you are ready for structure, a guided plan removes the guesswork — it tells you exactly how far and how easy to go each day, and ramps the volume safely so you never have to wonder if you are doing too much.
