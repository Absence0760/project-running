## Trail running is a different sport in the same shoes

If road running is about rhythm and pace, trail running is about adaptation. The ground is uneven, the gradients are steeper, and the pace is dictated by the terrain, not your legs. Road runners often arrive expecting their usual splits and are baffled to be much slower — that is normal and correct. On the trail, **effort, not pace, is the metric that matters.**

## Read the terrain

- **Look ahead, not down.** Scan the ground a few metres in front of you so you can pick your line, rather than staring at your feet.
- **Shorten your stride** over rough or technical ground. Quick, light steps keep you balanced where a long road stride would trip you.
- **Hike the steep climbs.** Even elite trail runners walk the steepest sections — power-hiking uphill is efficient, not a failure. Save the running for where it is faster than walking.
- **Descend relaxed.** Let gravity help, keep your knees soft, and don't fight the slope. Tension is what causes falls.

## Kit that differs from the road

- **Trail shoes** have aggressive lugs for grip on loose, wet, or muddy ground. Road shoes slip; trail shoes bite.
- **Carry water and a snack** on anything longer than a short loop — there are no shops or fountains in the hills.
- **A phone and a layer.** Weather changes faster off-road and you may be far from help. A light waterproof packs down to nothing and can save a day.

## Stay safe off-road

The freedom of the trail comes with more responsibility than a lap of the park.

- **Tell someone your route and expected return time**, especially if running alone.
- **Know where you are going.** Carry a map or a GPS route on your watch or phone — it is easy to take a wrong fork when everything looks like trees.
- **Respect the conditions.** Mud, ice, river crossings, and fading light all raise the stakes. Turn back if it stops being fun and starts being risky.

The best way to start is on well-marked, popular trails near you. Browse routes others have run and explore the paths near you to find a gentle first off-road loop — then build up as your footing and confidence grow.
