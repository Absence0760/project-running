## Why pre-run fuelling matters

Short, easy runs need no special preparation — you can roll out the door on an empty stomach and be fine. The long run is different. Once you are out for an hour or more, what you ate beforehand decides whether you finish feeling strong or fade and bonk halfway. Getting it roughly right is not complicated, and it makes a dramatic difference.

## Timing

The single biggest lever is *when* you eat, not exactly what.

- **A full meal** wants two to three hours of digestion before you run. Eat it, then let your stomach settle.
- **A small snack** can go in 30 to 60 minutes before, if you cannot fit a full meal in.
- **Right before running** is the worst window — food sitting undigested in your gut is what causes the dreaded mid-run stitch and stomach trouble.

Find the timing that works for you in training, never on race day.

## What to eat

Aim for **carbohydrate-led, low-fat, low-fibre, moderate-protein**. Carbohydrate is the fuel your muscles reach for first, and it digests cleanly. Good pre-long-run choices: oats, toast with honey or jam, a banana, a bagel, rice. These give you accessible energy without sitting heavy.

For runs beyond about 90 minutes, plan to take on carbohydrate *during* the run too — a gel, chews, or a sports drink every 30 to 45 minutes keeps your tank from running dry.

## Hydration

Drink to thirst in the hours before — being well hydrated at the start is the goal, not drowning yourself at the line. A glass or two of water with your pre-run meal is plenty for most. On hot days or very long efforts, carry fluid and sip regularly rather than gulping a lot at once.

## What to avoid

- **High-fat and high-fibre foods** (fry-ups, creamy sauces, big salads, beans) — they digest slowly and cause stomach distress.
- **Anything new.** Race morning is not the time to try an unfamiliar gel or breakfast. Rehearse your fuelling on training long runs.
- **Too much, too late.** A heavy meal an hour before the run will sit in your gut and slow you down.

Dialling this in is a matter of a few experiments. Logging what you ate and how the run felt makes the pattern obvious fast — you will quickly learn the breakfast and timing that leaves you feeling light and fuelled.
