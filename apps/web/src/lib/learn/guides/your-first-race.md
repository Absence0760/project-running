## Pick a race worth your first time

Your first race should be a celebration, not a trial. A few things make one beginner-friendly:

- **A distance you can already cover.** A 5K is the perfect first race — long enough to feel like an achievement, short enough that finishing is well within reach of anyone who has done a Couch to 5K.
- **A welcoming atmosphere.** Free weekly 5K events and community fun-runs are built for first-timers and have none of the intimidation of a big-city marathon.
- **A date far enough out** that you have weeks to prepare, not days.

## Finding an event

Local running clubs are the best on-ramp to your first event. They run regular group sessions, almost always know which nearby races suit beginners, and many host or organise events themselves. Joining a club — or just turning up to a free weekly 5K — surrounds you with people who were once exactly where you are, and removes the guesswork of finding a good first race on your own.

Browse the clubs and their events to see what is happening near you, and reach out — runners are, almost without exception, delighted to help a newcomer find their first start line.

## Signing up

Registration for an organised race is usually a short online form: your name, age, sometimes an estimated finish time, and a fee for paid events. You will typically receive a confirmation and, closer to the day, details about where to collect your race number (your "bib") and what time to arrive. Read the joining instructions carefully — knowing the start time, parking, and bag-drop in advance removes a lot of morning-of stress.

## What to expect on race day

- **Arrive early.** Give yourself time to park, find the start, use the toilet (the queues are long), and warm up.
- **Line up honestly.** Start toward the back if you are unsure of your pace — it is far nicer to overtake than to be swarmed.
- **Start slow** (see our guide on pacing your first race) and enjoy it. The crowd, the other runners, and the finish line are the whole point.
- **Soak up the finish.** Your first finish line is a genuine milestone. Whatever the time on the clock, you are now a runner who has raced.
