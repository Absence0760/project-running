## The idea behind Couch to 5K

Couch to 5K is the most successful beginner running plan ever written, and it works for one reason: it builds running fitness out of walking. Instead of asking an untrained body to run continuously — which it cannot yet do — it alternates short bursts of running with walking recovery, then slowly shifts the balance toward running over several weeks. By the end, the walking has quietly disappeared and you are running 5 km without stopping.

It is forgiving by design. If a week feels too hard, you repeat it. Nobody is timing you, and there is no failing.

## How a session is structured

Every session has the same three parts:

1. **Warm up** with a brisk five-minute walk. This is non-negotiable — it wakes up the muscles and the cardiovascular system so the running intervals do not come as a shock.
2. **The intervals.** A set of run/walk repeats. Early weeks might be "run 60 seconds, walk 90 seconds, eight times." Later weeks stretch the running and shrink the walking until the walking is gone.
3. **Cool down** with another easy five-minute walk to let your heart rate settle.

Do three sessions a week with a rest or easy day between each. Nine sessions a week is not nine times better — it is how you get injured.

## Pace: slower than you think

The number-one mistake is running the intervals too fast. The running portions should be slow — an easy, conversational jog, not a sprint. The goal of these weeks is not speed; it is teaching your body to keep moving. Speed comes later, and almost entirely on its own once the base is built. If you finish the running interval gasping, you went too hard. Ease off next time.

## Following it without thinking about it

The plan is simple but it asks you to remember a different interval pattern every week, and to keep track of where you are. That is exactly the kind of bookkeeping software is good at: a structured plan can lay out every session, count your intervals for you, and keep you honest about rest days — so all you have to do is show up and run.

By the end of the month, what felt impossible on day one — running for half an hour straight — is something you will simply do.
